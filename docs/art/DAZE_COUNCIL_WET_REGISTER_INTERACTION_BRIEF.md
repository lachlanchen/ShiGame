# Daze council wet-register hand-interaction brief

Status: Gate 5A source/native/import/package/runtime engineering admitted · watched visible-mesh release rejected · human review pending · not final prop/material, hand animation, historical reconstruction or close-camera approval

## Why this interaction exists

The first hand-performance slice belongs only to Chapter I node `rain-order`, with Chen Sheng as the canonical speaker. It makes one sentence physically legible:

> “If the register reaches the next post, we are nine hundred isolated offenders. If it does not, what are we?”

The words are SHI-authored dramatization, not a transcript. The received histories support the delayed conscripts, rain, expected missed deadline, Chen Sheng and Wu Guang, and the reported scale of nine hundred; they do not attest this council, this wording, the Keeper, or a surviving register. The interaction therefore stages a political problem without presenting an invented object or gesture as archaeological fact.

The dramatic action is restrained: Chen Sheng already supports the wet register in his left hand, brings his right hand onto its upper edge, holds the nine hundred names between both hands during the conditional question, then releases the right hand after “what are we?” The left hand never lets the register fall. The absence of an answer—not a flourish—completes the beat.

The campaign context begins with the Keeper/player holding the soaked register. Gate 5A therefore opens **after an offscreen pass** to Chen Sheng; it does not depict or imply that the Keeper never held it. A visible Keeper-to-Chen transfer would require two synchronized rigs, an explicit ownership transition and a separate story/camera contract. It is deferred rather than hidden inside this one-character contact proof.

This is the first concrete test of the project rule `ground → offer → regard → consequence`. It does not add dialogue, alter a choice, preselect an order, or make the animation authoritative.

## Reduced-motion and accessibility behavior

Reduced motion preserves the same five pieces of information as held poses/cuts: start, acquired contact, shared hold, ordered release and settled consequence. It suppresses continuous reach interpolation and never repeats. The prop cannot teleport between hands, so each cut must preserve ownership and screen direction. Skipping the beat goes directly to the exact terminal state and cannot preselect or issue a choice.

The reduced-v5 runtime reaches the ordered-release cut with semantic sample `90`, pose sample `91` and wrist-marker contact false. Its held-to-release frames differ, but full-frame visual review cannot read a confident mesh-level clearance: the fingers appear to touch or overlap the register. Reduced-motion information parity is therefore not accepted merely because the marker contract passes.

The object and contact must remain distinguishable without relying on skin tone, red/green coding, specular sparkle, motion blur or audio. Subtitles and the historical/dramatization disclosure remain readable at 200% text and in RTL layouts. Keyboard, controller and pointer paths may pause, skip or replay the presentation only through one shared semantic command; none may change campaign truth differently.
