# Broken Crossing environment look-development brief

Status: revision 2 approved for look development · not final art · historical-specialist review required · 2026-08-09

## Production need

The current Unreal command space proves rules, interaction, cinema, accessibility and package stability with deliberate engineering proxies. The next environment pass must give the player the feeling of standing inside a field command position during the collapse of Qin without hiding the six readable command signals or turning the scene into fantasy spectacle.

This brief authorizes one original 16:9 raster concept candidate for:

- Unreal environment and lighting look development;
- modular-set, material and prop planning;
- an internal/loading-screen candidate only after review.

It is not archaeological evidence, a final 3D asset, a marketing master or permission to infer historical facts from generated pixels.

## Scene and gameplay story

The position is a damaged river crossing after heavy rain in 209 BCE. A low, portable command table sits beneath an open reed-mat field shelter on the near bank. Beyond it, a shallow swollen channel, broken timber crossing pieces, wet reeds and a guarded route show the real operational problem. Distant moving points of fire imply pursuit without depicting a heroic battle.

The table is an instrument rather than decoration. Worked wood, restrained cord lines, wooden tally slips, small stone or bronze weights and one low earthenware oil lamp can suggest routes, loads, reserve, pursuit, cohesion and signals. They must remain physically distinct, readable silhouettes with calm negative space around them. They carry no written labels and do not pretend to be a literal historical map.

The viewpoint is the player's: standing-height, just behind the table, with the crossing and route aligned beyond it. Human figures, if present, remain a few distant practical silhouettes for scale; the concept must not establish unreviewed armour, rank insignia or named-character appearance.

## Visual target

- Material language: wet earth, worked dark wood, woven reed, oxidized bronze, stone, coarse cord, oil flame and water.
- Palette: charcoal and ink black; clay and dark wood; aged bronze focus; muted river jade; one restrained cinnabar warning accent.
- Lighting: storm-cleared blue-black ambient light, low warm oil light on the table, distant fire through rain haze; readable shadow detail rather than crushed black.
- Composition: cinematic wide frame, large calm areas, table as primary foreground instrument, river route as the middle-distance answer, pursuit as a small far signal.
- Finish: grounded cinematic realism with tactile materials and disciplined concept-art brushwork; no glossy fantasy-key-art finish.

## Historical and visual rejection list

Reject the candidate if any of the following is visible:

- paper map, printed page, modern diagram, compass rose or readable/generated text;
- pseudo-Chinese glyphs, decorative calligraphy or a banner used as exotic texture;
- glass-pane or hanging fantasy lantern, candle chandelier or modern lamp;
- stirrups, later lamellar stereotypes presented as established late-Qin fact, katana-like weapons or fantasy armour;
- tiled imperial-palace roof, mature Great Wall iconography, pagoda silhouette or later-period furniture;
- European siege equipment, barrels, crates with modern joinery, wheeled cannon or modern bridge hardware;
- magic glow, hologram, floating UI, excessive sparks/particles or game-board miniatures that turn people into toys;
- heroic battlefield charge, gore or anachronistic mass-uniform spectacle;
- watermark, logo, title or any other text.

## Translation constraints for Unreal

The concept is useful only if it can become a bounded modular set:

- table, shelter, bank, water/crossing, reeds and distant-pursuit layer must separate cleanly;
- the six signal silhouettes must stay legible behind Slate at 1400×900 and at the accepted council/command cameras;
- materials need plausible PBR translation rather than baked painterly detail doing all the work;
- lighting must retain the accepted opt-in audio, reduced-motion, focus, stencil and consequence-camera contracts;
- no visual element may become campaign authority or leak a later historical outcome.
